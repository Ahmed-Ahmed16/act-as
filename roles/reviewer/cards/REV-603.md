id: REV-603
rule: A named placeholder, such as :name, that 1 language's text has and another language's text lacks is a finding of translation check 3. Skip :count. It is never must fix and never fails health.
why: Laravel fills a placeholder by its name, so a text that lacks it shows without the value and nothing fails. :count is skipped because Laravel offers it as an optional built in placeholder for plural strings (the page says you may use it), and Arabic has its own plural forms for 0, 1 and 2, so its text may not carry the number.
applies-when: Running check T3 on a key whose text holds a placeholder in at least 1 language.
not-when: The only placeholder that differs is :count, or the names differ only in capital letters (Laravel treats :Name and :name as 1 placeholder).
example: In invented PR 101 for owner/shop, the en text is "Hello :name, order :order shipped" and the ar text for the same key holds :name only. Check 3 lists the key and says :order is missing in ar. The reviewer confirms it and writes a finding. It is not must fix.
mode: health, full
trigger: When running translation check T3 (a named placeholder missing in 1 language)

extra: The first page says a placeholder starts with a colon, and that a placeholder in all capitals or with a capital first letter gets a capitalized value, which is why :Name and :name count as 1. The second page names :count as the built in placeholder for plural strings. The CLDR chart gives Arabic its own plural categories zero, one and two. Its samples write 1 and 2 as words and 0 with a digit, so "Arabic writes 0, 1 and 2 in words" is true for 1 and 2 only. The firmer reason to skip :count is the Laravel wording "you may use"; the skip itself is the spec's rule. The tool compares names without case, so :Name and :name count as 1 placeholder there too (ticket 66). The finding word and never must fix come from the spec.
