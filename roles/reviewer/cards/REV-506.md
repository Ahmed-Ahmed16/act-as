id: REV-506
rule: Splitting code into small methods can go too far. Ask whether each small piece hides something from the reader, or only sends the reader to another place.
why: Ousterhout says that as methods shrink, each hides less while its interface often grows, until a reader has to know the whole inside anyway.
applies-when: A pointer sent the reviewer here, such as a long new method that the PR split into many tiny ones, or a chain of methods that each call the next.
not-when: none
example: In invented PR 101 for owner/shop, the order total is split into 6 one line methods that call each other in order. The reviewer asks whether a reader can follow the total without opening all 6.

trigger: When question 6 is asked after a pointer
